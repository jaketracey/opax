## When Parliament meets

Parliament does not meet all year. It meets in {{sitting weeks|sitting week}}. There are about 18 to 20 sitting weeks a year. [[f:sitting-weeks-18-20]]

A {{sitting day}} is a day a house meets. [[f:sitting-def]]

In a sitting week, members may start their day at 7 am and finish at 10 pm or later. When Parliament is not sitting, members spend much of their time in their electorates. [[f:sitting-hours]] [[f:not-sitting-electorate]]

## Question Time

{{Question Time}} is when members ask ministers questions. It happens in both houses at 2 pm on each sitting day, and usually lasts about an hour. [[f:qt-what]] [[f:qt-when]]

The first question always comes from the opposition. Then the government and the opposition take turns. Crossbench members also get to ask questions. [[f:qt-order]]

It gives members a chance to hold the government to account. [[f:qt-purpose]]

---screen---

## Two kinds of questions

In Question Time, ministers do not know what the opposition or crossbench will ask. These spoken questions are called {{questions without notice|question without notice}}. [[f:qwon-def]]

Government {{backbenchers|backbencher}} also ask questions. These are planned before Question Time and are called {{Dorothy Dixers|Dorothy Dixer}}. [[f:dorothy-dixer]]

A {{question on notice}} is written down. The minister answers it in writing. [[f:qon-def]] The minister is expected to answer within 30 days in the Senate, or 60 days in the House. [[f:qon-days]]

---screen---

## Watching Question Time

You can watch Question Time live, and it is recorded so anyone can watch or read it later. [[f:qt-watch]]

The OPAX Senate video page lists the Senate's own videos of Question Time and first speeches. [[f:opax-senate-video]]

OPAX links to the Senate's videos on YouTube. It does not cut or copy them. For the exact words spoken, the source is Hansard. [[f:opax-senate-video-rules]]

## Hansard

{{Hansard}} is the written record of what is said in Parliament. [[f:hansard-def]]

It covers the Senate, the House, the Federation Chamber and committee hearings. It also has the results of divisions and the text of petitions. [[f:hansard-covers]]

Editors take out mistakes and words like "um". But they cannot change the meaning of what was said. [[f:hansard-edits]]

---screen---

## How fast Hansard comes out

A first draft of Hansard, called the proof, goes online within 3 hours of a house finishing for the day. [[f:hansard-proof-3h]]

Members can suggest small fixes, such as the spelling of a name. They cannot rewrite what they said. [[f:hansard-corrections]]

The checked, official Hansard comes out around 2 weeks after each sitting week. [[f:hansard-official-2w]]

---screen---

## Other records of Parliament

Hansard records what is said. Two other records list what is done. In the Senate this is the Journals of the Senate. In the House it is called {{Votes and Proceedings}}. [[f:records-journals-vp]]

They list things like the bills brought in, every vote held and how each member voted. [[f:records-contents]]

Anyone can check these records to see what Parliament has decided. [[f:records-anyone]]

---screen---

## The rules and the agenda

Each house has its own rules. They are called {{standing orders}}. [[f:standing-orders-def]]

Each sitting day, each house also publishes a {{Notice Paper}}. It lists all the business waiting to be dealt with. [[f:notice-paper-def]]

These rules and lists help you know what a house is meant to do each day.

---screen---

## Committee hearings

Committees hold meetings called hearings, at Parliament House and around Australia. [[f:committee-hearings-where]]

Much of the work of committees is open for the public to watch, in person or online. [[f:watch-in-person-online]]

What is said at hearings goes into Hansard too. [[f:hansard-covers]] Speaking to a committee is one way to have your say on a bill or an issue. [[f:committee-submissions-hearings]]

---screen---

## Senate estimates

At {{Senate estimates}}, senators question ministers and public servants about how public money is used. [[f:estimates-def]]

Estimates hearings are held after the Budget, which usually comes in May. Extra hearings are held in October and February. [[f:estimates-timing]]

Hearings can run from 9 am to 11 pm. Any senator can ask questions. [[f:estimates-hours]] [[f:estimates-any-senator]]

---screen---

## Watching estimates

All estimates hearings are open to the media and the public. They can also be watched online. [[f:estimates-open]]

Hansard records everything that is said. [[f:estimates-hansard]]

OPAX has a Senate estimates page. It lists each round of hearings, and the transcripts by committee and day. A proof transcript can still be fixed before the official version comes out. [[f:opax-estimates-page]]

---screen---

## The press gallery and petitions

The {{press gallery}} is the group of media workers inside Parliament House. [[f:press-gallery-def]] They are always at Question Time to report on it. [[f:qt-press]]

A {{petition}} is a request from a group of people for Parliament to act on a problem. [[f:petition-def]]

You can also phone, write to or email any member of parliament at any time. [[f:contact-mps]]
