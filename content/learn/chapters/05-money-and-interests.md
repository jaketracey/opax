## Why money is disclosed

Parties, candidates and some other groups must tell the AEC about some of their money. The AEC is the body that runs our elections. [[f:aec-disclosure-returns]] [[f:aec-def]]

The aim is to make things more open. It lets the public see the money side of those who take part in elections. [[f:aec-disclosure-purpose]]

They do this by filling in forms. These forms are called returns. [[f:aec-disclosure-returns]]

---screen---

## Who has to tell the AEC

Some groups lodge an {{annual return}} each year. These include political parties, MPs and senators, and some donors. [[f:aec-annual-returners]]

Two other kinds of groups lodge them too: each {{associated entity}} and each {{significant third party}}. These groups must register with the AEC. [[f:aec-annual-returners]] [[f:registered-entities]]

Candidates and Senate groups lodge election returns after an election. [[f:aec-election-returners]]

---screen---

## The disclosure threshold

Not every amount has to be listed. Amounts at or below a set level do not have to be shown. This level is the {{disclosure threshold}}. [[f:threshold-def]]

From 1 July 2026 to 31 December 2026, the threshold is more than $17,300. [[f:threshold-2026]]

Until then, it goes up each 1 July, in line with prices. [[f:threshold-indexed]]

---screen---

## When returns are due

Parties, significant third parties and associated entities must lodge their annual return by 20 October each year. [[f:annual-return-20-oct]]

MPs and senators who receive gifts must lodge by 17 November. [[f:annual-return-17-nov]]

The AEC publishes annual returns on the first working day in February. They cover the financial year before. [[f:returns-published-feb]]

---screen---

## Where returns are published

The AEC puts returns on a website called the {{Transparency Register}}. Anyone can look at it. [[f:transparency-register-def]]

There are also returns for each federal election. They go online once the election is over. [[f:election-returns-published]]

For now, the AEC must put election returns online 24 weeks after polling day. This rule runs until the end of 2026. [[f:election-returns-24w]]

---screen---

## Gifts, donations and receipts

In election law, a donation means the same thing as a {{gift}}. [[f:donation-is-gift]]

But a return lists more than gifts. Its total receipts also include loans. [[f:loans-in-receipts]]

So OPAX calls this money "receipts", not "donations". A receipt is money a party or group received. It may or may not have been a gift. [[f:opax-receipts-language]]

---screen---

## Big changes from 2027

New funding and disclosure rules start on 1 January 2027. [[f:reform-start]]

The AEC says the disclosure threshold will change to "above $5,000". Returns will cover a calendar year, not a financial year. [[f:reform-threshold]] [[f:reform-calendar-year]]

Rules to help with the move to the new scheme apply from 1 July 2026. [[f:reform-transitional]]

---screen---

## Faster disclosure from 2027

Under the new rules, donations over the threshold must be reported quickly. [[f:reform-expedited]]

During an election period, the time limit is 7 days. Close to polling day, it can be as short as 24 hours. At other times, it is by the 21st day of the next month. [[f:reform-expedited]]

The AEC must then publish the details on its Transparency Register. [[f:reform-publish-24h]]

---screen---

## Caps on gifts and spending

From 2027 there are limits on gifts, called {{gift caps|gift cap}}. [[f:reform-gift-caps]]

The AEC says a person can give no more than $50,000 a year to one recipient for a federal purpose. They can give no more than $1.6 million in total. [[f:reform-gift-cap-amounts]]

There are also limits on {{electoral expenditure}}, which is money spent on elections. [[f:reform-expenditure-caps]] [[f:electoral-expenditure-def]]

---screen---

## Election funding

After each federal election or by-election, the AEC pays money to some parties and candidates. This helps cover what they spent on the election. [[f:election-funding-what]]

To get this {{election funding}}, a candidate or group needs at least four per cent of the first preference votes. [[f:election-funding-4pc]]

---screen---

## Registers of interests

MPs and senators list their interests on a {{register of interests}}. [[f:opax-declared]]

On OPAX, the Declared interests page shows the newest things added to and taken off these registers, grouped by week. Each entry points to the register it came from, so you can check the original. [[f:opax-declared]]

---screen---

## What OPAX shows

OPAX shows disclosed political receipts by source, party, industry and year. They come from AEC returns and some state electoral commission returns. [[f:opax-receipts-page]]

Federal and state returns are kept apart. [[f:opax-receipt-sources]]

A money link is not proof of anything wrong. As OPAX puts it, "An association does not prove influence." [[f:opax-no-influence]]

---screen---

## What OPAX cannot show

OPAX can only show what has been disclosed. Amounts at or below the threshold do not have to be disclosed. [[f:threshold-def]]

A return only appears once the AEC publishes it, which for annual returns is each February. [[f:returns-published-feb]]

Some AEC rows have been changed since OPAX copied them, and OPAX notes these are still being checked. [[f:opax-amended-rows]]

---screen---
