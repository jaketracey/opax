## Voting is compulsory

In Australia, all citizens who can vote must, by law, {{enrol}} and vote. [[f:compulsory-enrol-vote]]

You must be 18 or older to vote. If you are 16 or 17, you can enrol early, so you are ready to vote when you turn 18. [[f:enrol-16-17]]

Voting has been compulsory for more than 100 years. Most countries do not make people vote. [[f:compulsory-100-years]]

---screen---

## The electoral roll

The {{electoral roll}} is the list of everyone who can vote. [[f:electoral-roll-def]]

Australia has one national roll, run by the AEC. [[f:single-roll]]

At the end of 2024, around 98% of Australians who could enrol were on it. [[f:enrolment-98]] Getting on the roll is called enrolling. For an election, the roll closes seven days after the writ is issued. [[f:roll-closes]]

---screen---

## When elections happen

A full {{federal election}} is held about once every three years. The Governor-General sets the date when the government asks. [[f:election-every-3]]

The House's term ends three years after it first meets. But it can be closed sooner, usually on the Prime Minister's advice. [[f:house-expiry]]

At most elections, you vote for your House member and for half of your state's senators. [[f:election-every-3]]

---screen---

## The steps of an election

An election starts with a {{writ}}. This is a formal order to hold an election. It sets the key dates. [[f:writ-def]]

Writs are issued within 10 days of the House being dissolved. [[f:writ-10-days]]

The roll closes at 8 pm, seven days after the writ is issued. [[f:roll-closes]]

Election day must be a Saturday, at least 33 days after the writs are issued. [[f:polling-saturday]]

---screen---

## Two ballot papers

At most federal elections you get two {{ballot papers|ballot paper}}. [[f:two-ballots]]

The green one is for the House of Representatives. The white one is for the Senate. [[f:ballot-colours]]

On the green paper you vote for one person to represent your electorate. On the white paper you vote for senators for your state or territory. [[f:two-ballots]]

---screen---

## Preferential voting

Australia uses {{preferential voting}}. You number the candidates in the order you like them. [[f:preferential-def]]

On the green House paper, you must number every box. [[f:house-number-every-box]]

To win a House seat, a candidate needs more than half of the formal votes. [[f:house-50pc]] A formal vote is one that is filled in the right way, so it counts. [[f:informal-def]]

---screen---

## How House votes are counted

First, the counters add up everyone's number 1 votes. [[f:first-preference-count]]

If nobody has more than half, the candidate with the fewest votes is knocked out. Their votes go to the next candidate each voter numbered. This keeps going until someone has more than half. [[f:preference-transfer]] [[f:house-50pc]]

A ballot paper that is not filled in correctly is not counted. It is called an {{informal vote}}. [[f:informal-def]]

---screen---

## Voting for the Senate

Each Senate contest elects more than one senator. [[f:senate-multiple]]

On the white paper, you can vote above the line or below it. {{Above the line}}, you number at least 6 boxes for parties or groups. {{Below the line}}, you number at least 12 boxes for single candidates. [[f:atl-6]] [[f:btl-12]]

To win a Senate seat, a candidate needs a set share of the votes, called a {{quota}}. [[f:quota-def]]

---screen---

## Half-Senate elections

A state senator's term is 6 years. So at most elections, only half of the state seats are up. This is a {{half-Senate election}}. [[f:half-senate-def]] [[f:senate-terms]]

Senators for the two territories have 3-year terms. So their seats are up at each election. [[f:senate-terms]]

All senators face the voters at once only after a double dissolution. [[f:dd-all-senators]]

---screen---

## By-elections

A {{by-election}} fills one seat in the House between elections. It is held if a member resigns, dies or is found to be ineligible. [[f:by-election-def]]

The Speaker issues the writ. Polling must be on a Saturday. [[f:by-election-speaker]]

Voters in that one electorate go to the polls. The rest of the country does not vote. [[f:by-election-def]]

---screen---

## When a senator leaves early

The Senate works differently. If a senator resigns, dies or becomes ineligible before their term ends, there is a {{casual vacancy}}. [[f:casual-vacancy-def]]

The parliament of that state or territory chooses who fills the seat. If the senator was elected for a party, the new senator must come from the same party. There is no new vote by the public. [[f:casual-vacancy-fill]]

---screen---

## Redrawing electorates

From time to time, electorate lines are redrawn. This is a {{redistribution}}. It keeps the number of voters in each electorate about the same. [[f:redistribution-def]]

A redistribution can start if a state's number of seats changes. It can also start if seven years have passed since the last one. [[f:redistribution-triggers]]

An electorate's name can only change during a redistribution. [[f:electorate-names]]
