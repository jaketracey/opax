OPAX

What politicians say, on the record, searchable and citable.

OPAX cross-references what Australian politicians say in parliament with who funds them. Ask a question of half a million parliamentary speeches — federal House and Senate, committee hearings, and four state parliaments — and get an answer grounded in the record, with every claim cited to the actual speech.

      What OPAX is

      OPAX — the Open Parliamentary Accountability eXchange — exposes governance blind spots by cross-referencing what politicians say on the record with the money that flows around them. A politician who gives two hundred speeches about a problem but votes against every reform, or an industry that donates millions while its regulation stalls, leaves a paper trail. OPAX makes that trail searchable and citable.

      What you can do here

      • Ask — put a question to the whole corpus and get a grounded answer. Every claim is cited; every citation opens the actual speech at the passage it came from.
      • Search — hybrid semantic + keyword search with reranking, across speeches, with each result linked to the full document.
      • Reports — standing investigations pairing the structured money data (AEC donations classified by industry) with what was actually said in parliament.

      The speech corpus is filtered to the era of currently serving parliamentarians (March 1993 onward), deduplicated across overlapping sources, and stripped of procedural noise — division roll-calls, chair interjections, day indexes — so retrieval surfaces substance, not housekeeping. Voting records, government contracts and the disconnect-scoring analytics from the previous version of this site are being rebuilt on the new platform.

      How answers are produced

      Retrieval runs hybrid semantic and keyword search with a reranking pass over the corpus; a language model then writes the answer only from the retrieved passages, with numbered citations back to each source speech. If the record is thin on a question, the answer says so. OPAX is open source (AGPL-3.0).
