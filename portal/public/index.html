                What OPAX is

                OPAX, the Open Parliamentary Accountability Exchange, brings together public records about Australian politics and government. Parliamentary debate, public money, disclosures and official reports can be read beside one another, with links to the publishers so you can check the evidence.

                OPAX contains the names and images of people who have died.

                What you can do here

                Ask a question across the available records, or search and filter them. Answers cite the passages or structured records used. Browse a bill, person, organisation or report to follow its sources. The money map, Programs & places and interactive tools offer other ways into the record.

                What OPAX holds

                • Parliament. Hansard and committee evidence, recorded divisions, federal bills and their original text versions, sitting weeks, Senate estimates, and official government transcripts and releases. Senate video links Question Time, first speeches and other recordings from the Senate's own channel.
                • Money. Disclosed political receipts, campaigners and associated entities, party debts and benefits, Commonwealth and Queensland grants, Commonwealth contracts, and Budget papers and published figures. Charity and tax transparency data appear on matched organisation profiles.
                • People and interests. Parliamentarians, parties and electorates, with speeches, votes, federal pay and work expenses where held; declared interests; and matched ministerial diary, lobbyist and Foreign Influence Transparency Scheme records. Election histories and Census context have their own coverage limits.
                • Official reports and inquiries. Royal commissions and comparable inquiries, integrity body reports, Commonwealth annual report tables and agency annual reports. Report records link findings, recommendations, published responses, hearing transcripts and quoted figures to their sources. Agency figures retain PDF page numbers and mark OCR; their dedicated research tool is available through MCP. Queensland audit reports have a separate catalogue.
                • Registers. Tasmanian and ACT bill metadata, federal legislative instrument metadata, Victorian ministerial gifts, ACT notifiable invoices, Victorian government advertising, Treasury tax expenditures, ABS government finance and historical Grants SA projects.

                Coverage varies by collection, jurisdiction and year. Some collections contain selected documents or metadata only. Sources & coverage gives counts, publishers, dates, reuse terms and refresh arrangements.

                Reading the record

                A disclosed payment, shared name or meeting does not establish influence or misconduct. Hearing evidence and allegations are not findings. A commission's findings are not criminal convictions. A missing record does not establish that an event did not happen.

                Machine summaries and generated answers are reading aids. Verify quotations and figures against the linked primary source before publishing them. Methods explains extraction, source restrictions, known gaps and how to cite the record.

                Who makes it

                OPAX is an independent, non-partisan project of Jake Tracey, of Noice. Noice Pty Ltd publishes OPAX, including the iPhone and iPad app. The speech retrieval uses Progress Agentic RAG; the portal shares work with CorpusKit.

                Licences and reuse

                OPAX's code is open source under AGPL-3.0. Source material keeps its publisher's terms. There is no single licence covering the record: parliamentary copyright, Creative Commons grants, document exceptions and publication orders differ. Check the source attribution and reuse note for the material you intend to use. No publisher endorsement is implied.

                How to reach us

                Email support@opax.com.au for help with OPAX, and privacy@opax.com.au for anything about your personal information. Data errors are the corrections we most want to hear about. To report one in public, ask about the methodology, or point out a source we have missed, you can also open an issue on the GitHub repository, where the code, the collection scripts and the issue tracker all live in one place.                 How OPAX works, and how to cite it

                What OPAX is, and is not

                OPAX is a retrieval and analysis layer over public records. It is not the official record. Before publishing a quote, verify the wording at the source link on the document page. Every document links to its official origin where one exists.

                Corpus construction

                Speeches are included from 13 March 1993, the election that seated the longest-serving current federal parliamentarian, so nothing in the corpus predates a currently serving member. Speeches under 200 characters are excluded (procedural fragments), duplicates across overlapping sources are removed, and procedural noise (division roll-calls, chair interjections, day indexes) is stripped. The machine-readable corpus manifest, including per-source counts and the current corpus version, is at corpus.json.

                Speaker attribution

                Some earlier OpenAustralia transcripts combine several speakers' words in one document. These mixed transcripts are not credited to any one member or included in their speech count until each person's words can be attributed reliably. A fuller split into individual speaking turns is planned; a person page's speeches section says how many earlier transcripts record that member alongside other speakers. Chair-role transcripts record a member presiding over proceedings and are not counted as that member's own speeches.

                Retrieval and generation

                Speech search combines semantic and keyword retrieval with reranking. Ask can also use structured record tools for counts, financial tables, legislation, disclosures and official reports. Answers cite the retrieved passages or records used; a ranked sample does not establish a total. Embeddings: multilingual-2024-05-06. Generation model (pinned): DeepSeek V4 Pro via OpenRouter preset @preset/opax-pro (openai-compatible). The Ask research loop uses deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash via OpenRouter. Model identity may change between corpus versions and is recorded in the manifest.

                Reproducibility

                Searches are reproducible given a query, filters and corpus version: every export file embeds all three. Generated answers are not reproducible and must not be quoted as data. The supported method for a paper: state the question, cite the returned documents by their permanent OPAX links, and archive the export file.

                Citing instruments and audit reports

                Federal legislative instruments are a separate metadata catalogue. Cite the FRL id and the authoritative version linked on its page. OPAX shows metadata only, with one API-returned version per title, which may be historical. Current/latest flags are retained as published; version start, registration and commencement are distinct dates. Source: Federal Register of Legislation (legislation.gov.au), CC BY 4.0.

                Queensland audit reports carries the report index and the Queensland Audit Office's numbered recommendations. Cite the report number and year, the tabled date, and its authoritative QAO link. Source: Queensland Audit Office, CC BY 4.0, unless otherwise noted. © The State of Queensland (Queensland Audit Office) 2026. Copyright exceptions are recorded per report and their bodies withheld.

                Official reports and inquiries

                Official reports and report search bring together inquiry, integrity, Commonwealth annual-report and agency-report records. Each document is identified and cited to its publisher. An inquiry record groups its related documents; Commonwealth annual reports have a record per body and reporting year. Other report records represent individual documents. Records without a usable source citation are excluded.

                Each record has a permanent /report/<slug> page and JSON at /api/report/<slug>. Cite the document's title, publisher, reporting year or date, and page or table locator, with the official URL. OPAX's record is a route to that source. Findings and opinions keep the body's own wording and labels; allegations, submissions and investigation scope are not findings. A commission's finding is not a criminal conviction. Government responses and implementation statuses appear only where published, in the publisher's own terms. An absent status means unknown.

                Commission hearing transcripts

                Hearing transcripts are evidence, submissions and questioning, not findings. Listings retain the publisher's hearing dates, page numbers, redacted editions and document links. They cover the hearings supplied so far. Full text is available only for commissions whose publisher permits it under CC BY 4.0: Robodebt, Antisemitism and Social Cohesion, and NSW Healthcare Funding. Publisher restrictions and suppression or non-publication orders are respected. A transcript recording such an order or a pseudonymous witness is linked by hearing and page without quotations. Other transcripts remain metadata and publisher links. Report pages show a hearing section where listed; their JSON provides paged transcript links.

                Quoted figures from agency reports

                Agency annual-report figures come from the publisher's PDF, checked against its SHA-256. Each figure retains a verbatim quotation, PDF page number, reporting period, value, unit and the body's own label. Staff headcount and full-time equivalent, remuneration bands and actual amounts, and financial and operational measures remain distinct. Scanned documents read with OCR are marked; check the page image before quoting. A missing or unreadable figure remains unknown. The intake is a selection of reports, not every agency in each jurisdiction.

                These figures can be browsed through report search and read with the agency_reports MCP tool. That dedicated tool is not currently offered to Ask. Commonwealth PDFs that identify a Transparency Portal body contribute operational figures here so the structured pay, staffing and finance tables are not duplicated.

                Commonwealth annual report tables

                The Department of Finance's Transparency Portal supplies published tables. Earlier years use the Portal's dated data files; later years use its structured annual-report tables. Rows retain the body, reporting year, source table or CSV line and reuse terms. Non-corporate Commonwealth entity material is CC BY 4.0; corporate entities and companies retain copyright.

                Key management personnel remuneration is an accrual amount for the time in a role, not an annual salary. Executive bands contain counts and averages, not named individuals; averages are not added or multiplied across bands. Supplier figures retain the source unit, which can be dollars or thousands of dollars depending on year. Headline expenses exclude administered items, and staff tables report headcounts. OPAX does not audit or adjust the published figures. Coverage gives the different year ranges for each table series.

                Registers and public finance

                Tasmanian and ACT bills and federal instruments carry registry facts and original-version links. A registry stage is not a commencement date. Senate videos play from the Senate's YouTube channel after a click; OPAX does not download or rehost them.

                ACT invoice records are paid-invoice disclosures for invoices valued at $25,000 or more, not all spending or a count of unique invoices. Contracts, grant awards and payments are different measures. Advertising retains published table bases and units; tax expenditures are revenue forgone estimates, not cash payments. ABS government finance keeps published sector totals and function tables separate to avoid double counting. Budget figures retain their vintage and estimate, projection or actual labels. These reviewed snapshots are dated; a nightly build does not make them new source data.

                Victorian election 2026

                The election pages bring together official election facts and the records OPAX already holds. They show records only: no predictions, polls or rankings.

                • Districts, dates and results. From the Victorian Electoral Commission (VEC), checked 10 and 13 October 2026. The 88 districts and 8 regions are those drawn in the 2021 redivision, which took effect on 1 November 2022; no boundary change has been made since. Eureka was called Buninyong, and North-Eastern Metropolitan Region was called Eastern Metropolitan Region, before then.
                • Winning margins. The winner's share of the two-candidate vote above 50%, as the VEC's winning-margin maps use it. The share comes from the VEC's final count after preferences, or from its two-candidate count where the winner had a majority before preferences ran out. A district that has held a by-election or supplementary election since 2022 shows that later result as well.
                • Which seat am I in. Suburbs, postcodes and council areas are matched to districts with Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) allocation files (ASGS Edition 3: state electoral divisions 2025, suburbs and localities 2021, postal areas 2021, local government areas 2025), joined by mesh block and weighted by usual residents at the 2021 Census. Postcodes are the ABS's postal areas, which approximate delivery postcodes. Where a place crosses district lines the page gives each district's share of its residents; the VEC's address search gives the exact answer.
                • Members. The Parliament of Victoria's member list as at 9 September 2026, with each member's party as listed there. Each member links to their existing OPAX profile.
                • Speeches. Counts of Victorian Hansard speeches held by OPAX, with the years they cover. A count that starts in 2022 includes speeches made before the November 2022 election. Members who have also sat in federal Parliament show no count, because their records can't yet be separated.
                • Votes. Victorian division records cover 2026 only, and only questions with a recorded title are listed. A missing record doesn't mean a member didn't speak or vote.
                • Not covered. There is no Victorian register of members' interests in OPAX and no Victorian state grants. Federal electorates are different from state districts, so federal grants aren't assigned to them. No donor records appear on the election pages.
                • Licences. VEC website material and ABS data are licensed CC BY 4.0 (© Victorian Electoral Commission; Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics). No VEC maps, images or branding are reproduced. Victorian Hansard and division records are copyright material without an open licence, so these pages show counts and links, not the text of speeches.

                electorates_rank

                Rank, filter or look up federal or state electorate demographics from the published 2021 Census (ABS). Ask and MCP use the same tool.                 • Required: indicator.                 Reading the record

                On the website

                You can read, search and ask without an account. OPAX runs on Cloudflare, so every request your browser makes reaches Cloudflare's network with your IP address, as a request to any website does.

                OPAX uses the address to limit how often one address can ask questions (20 a minute), ask for suggested follow-up questions and search summaries (20 a minute), search (120 a minute) and draw share images (60 a minute). Cloudflare counts these per address over 60-second windows. OPAX also refuses some requests from networks that run automated scraping.

                Sign-in, account and voice requests have limits of their own. For these, OPAX stores a counter under a SHA-256 hash of the address and the time window, not under the address itself. Expired counters are deleted during later sign-in requests, a day or more after their window ends.

                Requests that OPAX's server handles are logged in Cloudflare Workers Logs. They include page loads, searches, questions, other requests for data the server works out, and analytics events passed on to PostHog. Each log entry records the address requested, which for a search includes the search words. Files the site serves as they are, such as scripts, styles and most published data files, come straight from Cloudflare's network without reaching the server, so they are not in these logs. On OPAX's Cloudflare plan the logs are kept for 7 days, as Cloudflare documents, and then deleted. Cloudflare may record connection details, such as your IP address, with each entry.

                Cloudflare's own traffic and security analytics for opax.com.au also record requests with their IP addresses. Cloudflare documents that it keeps these request-level records for up to 31 days.

                Questions and searches

                Where the words you type go depends on what you are doing.

                • Searches of OPAX's catalogs, such as people, parties, donors, contracts, grants, declared interests, pay and expenses, are looked up on OPAX's own server. The search words go to no other service.
                • Searches of the record's documents, including the standard search of everything and searches of bill text, also send the search words and filters to Progress Agentic RAG, the service that holds the record and finds the passages that match.
                • Search summaries. Unless you have turned summaries off, a search for words also asks for a short summary of the records it found, even when it searched only the catalogs. The search words, filters and those records go through Progress to a language model reached through OpenRouter.
                • Questions on the Ask page and in a conversation go to Progress, which finds passages, then with those passages to the language model through OpenRouter. Follow-up questions carry the conversation's earlier turns with them.

                Searches made by the voice assistant and by research tools follow the same paths. OPAX's code adds no account details or other identifier to these requests. Progress can still receive your IP address with them: Cloudflare, which runs OPAX's server, documents that it adds the visitor's IP address to requests the server makes to services outside Cloudflare, and Progress is outside it. OpenRouter is called by Progress, not by OPAX's server.

                What these services keep, and for how long, is set by their own terms: Progress's privacy policy, OpenRouter's privacy policy, and the policy of the provider OpenRouter sends the request to.

                So that a repeated question needs no new work, Cloudflare keeps copies of answers to opening questions, search results and summaries for between ten minutes and seven days. The copies are filed by the question, not by who asked it.

                Stored in your browser

                The website keeps a few things in your browser, and they last for different times.

                • Two preferences stay until you clear this site's data: the kind of question you last chose in the question builder, and that you turned search summaries off. Turning summaries back on removes the second.
                • Two things last only as long as the open tab: how you were reading a search, and the question and answer you carry from Ask into a conversation. That question and answer is removed as soon as the conversation opens.
                • Your most recent conversations with OPAX, up to 20, so that you can return to them. Older ones drop off as new ones arrive, sooner if they take up too much space. You can delete a conversation from the conversation list; while you are signed in, that deletes the copy in your account too. If you sign in on the website, conversations are copied to your account, including ones saved before you signed in; see Community accounts.

                Clearing this site's data in your browser removes what the browser holds, but not the copies in your account. The analytics cookies are described next.

                Analytics on the website

                The website uses Google Analytics, loaded through Google Tag Manager, and PostHog to count visits and see which features are used. Both receive the page's address with any search terms and fragments removed, and a fixed list of events: a question or search started, finished or failed, with its duration and result counts; a source opened; a download; a link followed to another site; and similar actions. They never receive question text, search words, answers, page titles or postcodes. Campaign tags in a link are kept only as a plain source, medium and campaign name; other tags and advertising click identifiers are dropped.

                PostHog also receives anonymous error reports when something on a page goes wrong in your browser. A report gives the kind of error, a shortened error message and the place in OPAX's own code where it happened. Before a report is sent, any quoted text is removed from the message, and search terms and fragments are removed from any address in it. Errors from browser extensions or other sites' scripts are not sent. Google Analytics receives no error reports.

                With each event, both services also receive details your browser shares, such as your browser and its version, operating system, device type, screen and window size, language and time zone. PostHog also receives the domain of the site that linked you to OPAX, but not the rest of its address.

                Each service stores an anonymous identifier in your browser so that a returning browser and a visit can be counted. Google Analytics sets cookies whose names begin with _ga, which Google documents as lasting two years. PostHog keeps its identifier in a cookie that lasts a year and in local storage, which stays until you clear it. Neither builds a profile of you: PostHog's person profiles, session recording and surveys are off, and Google signals and advertising personalisation are off.

                PostHog's cookie also records the full address you first arrived at, including any search words in it. Like every cookie for opax.com.au, it travels with your requests to OPAX's server, which does not use it and does not pass it on. OPAX removes that address from the events PostHog receives.

                Your browser sends Google Analytics events straight to Google, so Google receives your IP address. PostHog events pass through OPAX's server, which forwards your IP address to PostHog's servers in the United States, so that PostHog places visits by your location rather than by the server's. PostHog may store the address with the event.

                Both services keep the events they receive for the retention period of OPAX's account with them, under their own terms: Google's privacy policy and PostHog's privacy policy.

                If your browser sends a Do Not Track signal, neither service receives anything, though PostHog may still store its identifier in your browser. Analytics run only on opax.com.au, and community pages load none at all.

                The grants map loads its map tiles from OpenStreetMap's servers, which receive your IP address.

                In the iPhone and iPad app

                The OPAX app for iPhone and iPad needs no account to read, search or ask. It has no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It sends no device or advertising identifier, and it contains no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising code from other companies. Its requests go only to opax.com.au, with your device's IP address, under the same limits and logs as the website (see Reading the record). Pages the app opens on opax.com.au are the website, and the website sections above apply to them.

                • Email Address (contact info): the address you sign in with, kept with your account until you delete it. See Community accounts.
                • User ID (identifiers): your account's member ID, which sits behind your sign-in session and on your voice records and saved conversations.
                • Audio Data (user content): your voice during a Talk call, which streams through OPAX's server to ElevenLabs. OPAX keeps no recording; see Talking to OPAX by voice.
                • Other User Content (user content): the questions you ask in Ask, which go to Progress and a language model reached through OpenRouter once you agree, and, while you are signed in, the conversations copied to your account; and the words of a Talk call, spoken or typed, which go to ElevenLabs.
                • Product Interaction (usage data): for each voice call, the seconds reserved and charged and when it started and ended, which count your 10 minutes.
                • Search History: the words you search for, which travel in the request's address and so are kept in OPAX's server logs for 7 days. Searches of the record's documents also go to Progress.
                • Other Diagnostic Data (diagnostics): OPAX's server logs record each request's address, time and outcome and are kept for 7 days. Cloudflare may also record your IP address with a log entry. Cloudflare's traffic and security analytics record requests with their IP addresses for up to 31 days. Rate limits also read your IP address. OPAX uses this data only for security, to limit abuse and to keep the service running.

                What the app sends, and where

                • Searches of OPAX's catalogs of people, places, bills, declared interests, pay and expenses are looked up on OPAX's own server. Searches of the record's documents and search summaries go on to Progress, and summaries to a language model, as under Questions and searches.
                • Ask. Before your first question is sent, Ask asks your permission: questions go to Progress and a language model reached through OpenRouter, which are third-party AI services, and nothing is sent until you agree. Your question, with the earlier turns of the conversation, then goes to OPAX's server and on to those services, with no account details. After an answer, Report this answer opens the support page with only the address of the record you chose, never your question or the answer. The app keeps your most recent conversations, up to 20, on your device. While you are signed in, each conversation is copied to your account after every answer, and opening Your conversations also copies the ones saved on the device before you signed in. An account holds up to 50. Deleting a conversation, or Delete all conversations, removes the copies in your account too.
                • Talk to OPAX works as described under Talking to OPAX by voice. Report this answer opens the support page with only the address of the record you chose, never what was said.
                • Community is on the website only. The app does not show the community or send anything to it: discussions, replies, messages, profiles, reading lists, likes and follows are written and kept only through opax.com.au, under Community accounts.

                What the app does not collect

                • Your location. If you use Use my location when picking your electorate, iOS asks for your permission, and the app then uses one reading of your location, on your device, to suggest an electorate. Your location is never sent to OPAX or anyone else, and is not stored: the app downloads every electorate's outline before it reads your location, so its requests are the same wherever you are, and only the electorate you confirm is saved. The app never asks for your location in the background.
                • What stays on your device: the seat you choose, the members, bills and electorates you follow, your quiz answers, your voice agreement, your captions setting, the records kept for offline reading, and your conversations with OPAX until you sign in. None of these is sent anywhere.
                • Nothing else: no messages, contacts, photos, health, financial or purchase details, crash reports or device identifiers. Source links open in a Safari view that the app cannot read, so it records no browsing history.

                The companies that receive data from the app are Cloudflare, ElevenLabs, Progress Agentic RAG and OpenRouter, under Who else receives data.

                Community accounts

                An account is optional. On the website it lets you keep reading lists, take part in discussions, message other members and connect research tools. If you sign in to the app, it is the same account: there it lets you talk to OPAX by voice and keep your conversations with OPAX in your account. The community itself is on the website only.

                What an account holds

                • Your email address, to sign you in and send the emails described below. It is never shown on your profile or to other members.
                • Your display name and a short bio, if you add them, the date you joined and your follower count. These are public.
                • What you write and save: discussions and replies, which are public; reading lists, which are private until you share them; saved discussions, which only you see; follows, likes and blocks; and messages to other members.
                • Saved conversations with OPAX. While you are signed in on the website or in the app, your conversations are copied to your account so that they appear on your other devices, including any that browser or device saved before you signed in. An account holds up to 50. Only you can see them.
                • Your settings: who can message you, and whether you receive reply emails.

                Direct messages are visible only to their two participants. OPAX stores them; they are not end-to-end encrypted. If you report a message, moderators can read that message and your reason. Likes, replies and follows can notify the member they concern. Blocking a member stops their messages, removes follows between you and hides each other's discussions while you are signed in.

                Signing in

                OPAX emails you a sign-in link and, when you sign in from the app, an eight-digit code as well. Each expires after 15 minutes and works once. OPAX stores only a hash of the link and a keyed hash of the code, with your email address, and deletes them a day or more after they expire, during later sign-in requests.

                Signing in starts a session that lasts up to 30 days. On the website it is held in a secure cookie that page scripts cannot read; if you sign in to the app, it is held in the device's Keychain, on that device only. OPAX stores a hash of the session token, the account it belongs to, when it started and ends, and whether it began on the website or in the app. It does not store your IP address or device details with a session. You can sign out on one device or on every device.

                Email

                Emails come from login.opax.com.au and are sent through Cloudflare's email service. OPAX sends sign-in emails, account deletion codes and, when someone replies to a discussion you started, a reply notification. You can turn reply emails off in Privacy & messages, and each one has an unsubscribe link. OPAX sends no newsletters or marketing email. Cloudflare's email service keeps its own delivery records, under Cloudflare's privacy policy.

                Research tools

                Members can create keys that let research tools search the record through OPAX's MCP service. Keys are stored as hashes, expire after 90 days, can be revoked at any time and record when they were last used. Searches made with a key go through the same search as the website. The MCP tool reference lists inputs and examples.

                Talking to OPAX by voice

                Talk to OPAX is an optional voice assistant for signed-in members. It answers from the same public record and links its sources. It is run by ElevenLabs, a third-party AI provider.

                Your microphone

                OPAX uses your microphone only during a call you start. On the website, opening the voice panel does not touch the microphone: it starts when you choose Start talking, and stops when the call ends, when you close the panel and when you leave the tab. If you use voice in the app, you agree to the terms in this section once, then iOS asks for microphone permission the first time you start a call. Your agreement is stored on your device; you can withdraw it at any time on the voice screen, and the app asks again before your next call. A call ends when you leave the app.

                What is sent, and to whom

                During a call, your voice streams through OPAX's server to ElevenLabs. ElevenLabs turns your speech into text, writes the replies with a language model that ElevenLabs runs for the assistant, and speaks them back. Anything you type during a call goes the same way. OPAX gives ElevenLabs a random identifier for the call and the call's time limit; its code does not give ElevenLabs your email address or account details. ElevenLabs can receive your IP address with the connection, because Cloudflare adds the visitor's IP address to requests that OPAX's server makes to services outside Cloudflare. When the assistant looks something up, its search runs through OPAX's own search, as described under Questions and searches.

                What is kept

                When OPAX last checked (9 September 2026), its voice assistant was set at ElevenLabs not to record audio and to delete transcripts after one day. ElevenLabs' own privacy policy also applies. OPAX itself keeps no recording and no transcript. The transcript you see during a call is not saved in your browser or in the app, and is never sent to analytics.

                For each call, OPAX keeps a record tied to your account: its state, the seconds reserved and charged, when it started and ended, and ElevenLabs' reference for the conversation.

                Your 10 minutes

                Each account has 10 minutes (600 seconds) of voice in total, not each month. All members also share a monthly budget of 40,000 seconds, and no more than two calls can run at once. The call records above are how OPAX counts all three.

                Starting a call reserves your remaining time, or what is left of the month's budget if that is less. Cancelling the reservation before OPAX starts connecting the call returns the time, and a reservation that is never used expires without charge. Once OPAX starts connecting the call, the time can stay charged if OPAX cannot confirm that no call ran or how it ended, for example when the connection to ElevenLabs fails partway or drops. A call that ends normally is charged for the seconds it ran. A few accounts approved by OPAX can make repeated calls of up to 10 minutes each.

                Deleting your account

                On the website, open Delete your account from your account page. In the app, tap the person icon (Account and about), then Delete account. OPAX emails you a fresh code, and entering it removes the account from OPAX's live database at once. Deletion cannot be undone. Anyone who can receive the account's email can do it, since both signing in and the deletion code go to that address.

                What is deleted

                • Your email address, display name, bio and settings, and any voice access OPAX gave your account.
                • Every session on every device, any unused sign-in links and codes, and your research tool keys.
                • Everything you wrote or saved: discussions, replies, reading lists, saved conversations, messages you sent, likes, bookmarks, follows, blocks, reports you made, and notifications to or from you.
                • Emails waiting to be sent to you, your unsubscribe links and the rate-limit counters tied to your account.

                What is kept, and why

                • Voice call records lose their link to your account but keep their seconds and times, because the shared monthly budget and the two-call limit depend on them; deleting them would hand back time already used. Each keeps ElevenLabs' reference for its conversation until OPAX's housekeeping, which runs every five minutes, clears it one day after the call was recorded as ended. Clearing the reference does not delete ElevenLabs' copy of the conversation, which follows the ElevenLabs settings above.
                • Other members' replies to a discussion you started stay, under a placeholder titled "Deleted discussion" with no text, link or date. A conversation you were part of stays readable by the other member, with their own messages only, and takes no new messages. You are shown as "Deleted account". What other members wrote is not changed, even where it quotes you.
                • Rate-limit counters for IP addresses stay until they expire.
                • Emails already sent cannot be recalled.
                • Recovery history. Cloudflare keeps a rolling history of OPAX's database so that it can be restored to an earlier point if something goes wrong. It is always on, and on OPAX's plan it covers the last 30 days, as Cloudflare documents. Your deleted data stays in it for up to 30 days, then is gone. OPAX runs no other scheduled backup or export of the database.

                After deletion, OPAX's live database keeps no record of your email address. Signing up again with it creates a new account, with a fresh 10 minutes of voice. The browser or app you delete from clears its saved conversations; other browsers keep their copies until you clear this site's data there, and the app on another device keeps its own until you delete them in Ask. The public record is not affected.

                Who else receives data

                • Cloudflare runs OPAX's servers, database, logs, rate limits and email, and handles every request with your IP address. OPAX's community database is held in Cloudflare's Oceania region.
                • Progress Agentic RAG receives questions, searches of the record's documents and the inputs to search summaries, as set out under Questions and searches, including those made by the voice assistant and by research tools. It can receive your IP address with them. OPAX's knowledge box is in its AWS Sydney zone.
                • OpenRouter, and the model provider it routes to, receives from Progress the questions, search words and passages needed to write answers and summaries.
                • ElevenLabs receives voice audio, messages typed during a call and the call's random identifier, and can receive your IP address with the connection. The language model ElevenLabs runs for the assistant receives the words of the conversation through ElevenLabs. OPAX uses ElevenLabs' standard service, not one of its regional data residency services, and ElevenLabs states that it stores this data in the United States, while processing can also happen elsewhere, through its affiliates and subprocessors. Its privacy policy applies.
                • Google, through Analytics and Tag Manager, receives page addresses, the events and browser details listed above and your IP address, on the website only.
                • PostHog receives the same events and browser details, and your IP address forwarded by OPAX's server, on the website only, in the United States.
                • OpenStreetMap receives your IP address when the grants map loads its tiles.
                • YouTube: on the website, a page with a Senate video loads its thumbnail image from YouTube (i.ytimg.com), the player loads from YouTube (youtube-nocookie.com) when you press play, and Google's privacy policy applies to the player. OPAX uses YouTube API Services to list the videos the Senate publishes, and watching them means agreeing to the YouTube Terms of Service. The app does not show these videos.
