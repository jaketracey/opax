        Methods · About OPAX
            
    
            

    
            
      
            What OPAX is
            
      
            OPAX, the Open Parliamentary Accountability eXchange, exposes governance blind spots by
      cross-referencing what politicians say on the record with the money that flows around them.
      A politician who gives two hundred speeches about a problem but votes against every reform,
      or an industry that donates millions while its regulation stalls, leaves a paper trail.
      OPAX makes that trail searchable and citable.
            

      
            What you can do here
            
      
              
        
            • Ask: put a question to the speech corpus (widen it to the
        recorded divisions with one checkbox) and get a grounded answer. Every claim is cited;
        every citation opens the actual speech at a stable, linkable page.
              • 
        
            • Search: hybrid semantic + keyword search with reranking, filterable
        by speaker, party, parliament and year, with citation-ready exports.
              • 
        
            • Reports: standing investigations pairing the structured money data
        (AEC donations classified by industry) with what was actually said in parliament.
              • 
        
            • Money map: the largest disclosed donors in three dimensions, ringed
        by industry around the parties they gave to, with a year range you can drag and a words
        layer showing who speaks on each industry's debates.
              • 
        
            • Encyclopedia: an entry for every parliamentarian, party, donor and
        topic, with quick facts, voting record, declared interests, expenses and the money map
        narrowed to that entity.
              • 
        
            • Explore: the time machine, the record quiz, the ledger, who owns
        which debate, words per dollar, and then versus now.
              • 
      
            

      
            The corpus
            
      
            
        
            SourceDocumentsCoverage
            
        
      
            
      
            The speech corpus is filtered to the era of currently serving
      parliamentarians (March 1993 onward), deduplicated across overlapping sources, and stripped
      of procedural noise (division roll-calls, chair interjections, day indexes) so retrieval
      surfaces substance, not housekeeping. Recorded divisions and per-member voting records are
      in; government contracts and the disconnect-scoring analytics from the previous version of
      this site are not yet rebuilt. Full detail: methods & how to cite
      OPAX.
            

      
            How answers are produced
            
      
            Retrieval runs hybrid semantic and keyword search with a reranking pass over the corpus;
      a language model then writes the answer only from the retrieved passages, with
      citations back to each source speech. If the record is thin on a question, the answer says
      so. OPAX is open source (AGPL-3.0).
            
    
            

    
    
            
      
            Methods
            
      
            How OPAX works, and how to cite it
            

      
            What OPAX is, and is not
            
      
            OPAX is a retrieval and analysis layer over public records. It is not the official
      record. Before publishing a quote, verify the wording at the source link on the document
      page. Every document links to its official origin where one exists.
            

      
            Corpus construction
            
      
            Speeches are included from 13 March 1993, the election that seated the
      longest-serving current federal parliamentarian, so nothing in the corpus predates a
      currently serving member. Speeches under 200 characters are excluded (procedural fragments),
      duplicates across overlapping sources are removed, and procedural noise (division
      roll-calls, chair interjections, day indexes) is stripped. The machine-readable corpus
      manifest, including per-source counts and the current corpus version, is at
      corpus.json.
            

      
            Retrieval and generation
            
      
            Search runs hybrid semantic + keyword retrieval with a reranking pass. Answers are
      generated only from retrieved passages, with citations. Embeddings:
      multilingual-2024-05-06. Generation model (pinned):
      gemini-2.5-flash-lite. Model identity may change between corpus versions and
      is recorded in the manifest.
            

      
            Reproducibility
            
      
            Searches are reproducible given a query, filters and corpus version: every export file
      embeds all three. Generated answers are not reproducible and must not be
      quoted as data. The supported method for a paper: state the question, cite the returned
      documents by their permanent OPAX links, and archive the export file.
            

      
            Known limitations
            
      
            
      
              
        
            • Hansard is a revised record ("substantially verbatim"), not a raw transcript.
              • 
        
            • Party labels use a 15-value canonical vocabulary; some speeches carry no party label.
              • 
        
            • State coverage windows differ by parliament (QLD only from 2024, NSW/VIC from 2015).

            Parliamentary expenses
            
      
            What the expense categories mean
            
      
            Every parliamentarian page on OPAX splits claimed expenses by category. Those
      category names are the Independent Parliamentary Expenses Authority's own, reproduced
      unchanged from its quarterly expenditure reports, so a figure here can be checked line by
      line against the published source. This page says what each one covers.
            
      
            Some of these are not expense types at all. IPEA records the purpose of
      a trip against transport lines, so labels such as parliamentary duties or electorate duties
      answer why the travel happened, not what was bought. Others are entitlements paid at a set
      rate rather than reimbursements of receipts. Both are marked below.
